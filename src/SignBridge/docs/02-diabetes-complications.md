# Diabetes — Complications and Medication Context

**Category:** endocrinology
**Condition:** diabetes
**Red flag:** false

## Common medications and what they do

- **Metformin** — first-line oral agent for type 2 diabetes; reduces hepatic glucose production. Watch for GI upset and (rarely) lactic acidosis.
- **Insulin (basal or rapid-acting)** — direct glucose control; risk is hypoglycemia.
- **GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide)** — weekly or daily injections; weight loss and cardiac benefit.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin)** — renal-protective; risk of euglycemic DKA.
- **Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glyburide)** — older agents; hypoglycemia risk.

## Why diabetic patients need extra triage care

- Diabetics often have **silent ischemia** — chest pain may be absent or atypical when an MI is happening.
- Long-standing diabetes raises baseline risk for: stroke, heart attack, kidney disease, retinopathy, peripheral neuropathy.
- A diabetic with new chest pain, dizziness, or shortness of breath is escalated one ESI tier above their stated complaint.
- Foot wounds in diabetics are urgent — risk of osteomyelitis and amputation.

## Pre-visit signals to surface

- Type (1 or 2) and years since diagnosis.
- HbA1c if known.
- Current insulin regimen vs. oral agents.
- Recent hypoglycemic episodes.
- Renal function (eGFR if known).

## Interpreter notes

The interpreter will hear terminology that is dense and rapid. Pre-load signs for: insulin, glucose, blood sugar, A1C, kidney, neuropathy, hypoglycemia, ketoacidosis. The patient may use brand names (Ozempic, Jardiance) — confirm with the patient that the sign matches their understanding.
