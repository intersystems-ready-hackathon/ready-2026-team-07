# Emergency Severity Index (ESI) 1–5 Rubric

**Category:** triage
**Condition:** rubric
**Red flag:** false

The ESI is a five-level triage algorithm widely used in U.S. emergency departments. SignBridge uses an ESI-style score to communicate urgency to both the doctor and the interpreter before the call.

## ESI 1 — Immediate, life-threatening

Patient requires immediate life-saving intervention. Examples:

- Cardiac arrest, respiratory arrest, severe respiratory distress.
- Active seizure, unresponsive patient, severe trauma with shock.
- Hypertensive emergency with end-organ damage.
- Anaphylaxis with airway compromise.

Interpreter must be ready immediately; brief should emphasize urgency and emergency vocabulary.

## ESI 2 — High-risk, must be seen quickly

High-risk situations, severe pain, confusion, or vital signs in danger zone. Examples:

- Suspected acute coronary syndrome (chest pain with red flags).
- Stroke symptoms within window for thrombolytics.
- Severe asthma exacerbation, peak flow < 40%.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis or severe hypoglycemia.
- Suicidal ideation with plan.

Interpreter brief should include red-flag findings and what the doctor is most likely to ask.

## ESI 3 — Multiple resources expected

Stable but will require multiple workups (imaging, labs, specialist consult). Examples:

- Abdominal pain in middle-aged adult, stable vitals.
- Moderate asthma exacerbation responding to inhaler.
- Laceration requiring evaluation for sutures plus tetanus update.

## ESI 4 — One resource expected

Stable, low complexity. Examples:

- Sprain, simple laceration, urinary infection symptoms.
- Mild asthma exacerbation, stable.
- Medication refill request with brief evaluation.

## ESI 5 — No resources expected

Routine evaluation. Examples:

- Suture removal, prescription refill, simple medication question.

## How to escalate

Bump the ESI level **one tier more urgent** when:

- Patient is diabetic and presenting with cardiovascular or neurological symptoms.
- Patient is over 65 with new neurological deficit.
- Patient is immunocompromised or post-transplant.
- Patient is pregnant with abdominal pain or bleeding.
