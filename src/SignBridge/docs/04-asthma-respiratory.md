# Asthma and Respiratory Distress — Pre-Visit Notes

**Category:** pulmonology
**Condition:** asthma
**Red flag:** false

## Severity assessment

- **Mild exacerbation:** dyspnea on exertion, peak flow > 70% personal best, responsive to inhaler.
- **Moderate:** dyspnea at rest with phrases not full sentences, peak flow 40–70%, partial inhaler response.
- **Severe (ESI 2):** unable to speak in phrases, accessory muscle use, peak flow < 40%, minimal inhaler response.
- **Life-threatening (ESI 1):** silent chest, cyanosis, altered mental status, peak flow < 25% — this is a red flag.

## When asthma is a red flag

- Patient unable to complete a sentence.
- Cyanosis (blue lips, fingertips).
- Altered consciousness or extreme fatigue.
- Recent ICU admission for asthma.
- More than three rescue-inhaler uses in the last 24 hours with poor response.
- Peak flow under 40% of personal best despite treatment.

## Common medications

- **Short-acting beta-agonists (albuterol)** — rescue inhaler, used as needed.
- **Inhaled corticosteroids (fluticasone, budesonide)** — daily controller.
- **Combination inhalers (Symbicort, Advair)** — controller + long-acting beta-agonist.
- **Oral steroids (prednisone)** — short bursts for exacerbations.
- **Biologics (omalizumab, mepolizumab)** — for severe allergic or eosinophilic asthma.

## Pre-visit signals to surface

- Number of rescue-inhaler uses in the last 24–48 hours.
- Peak flow reading vs. personal best.
- Trigger if known (URI, allergen, exercise, cold air).
- Recent steroid course.
- Smoking status (active or passive).

## Interpreter notes

Pre-load signs for: inhaler, peak flow, wheeze, shortness of breath, steroid, rescue medication. The patient may be physically distressed and unable to sustain long signed exchanges — interpreter should pace shorter exchanges and confirm comprehension frequently.
