# Chest Pain — Pre-Visit Triage Notes

**Category:** cardiology
**Condition:** chest_pain
**Red flag:** true

## When chest pain is a red flag

Chest pain in adults is treated as a red flag (emergency-tier) when any of these features are present:

- Crushing, pressure, or squeezing pain lasting more than 15 minutes.
- Pain radiating to the left arm, jaw, neck, or back.
- Associated diaphoresis (sweating), nausea, vomiting, or shortness of breath.
- Patient has known coronary artery disease, prior MI, or recent stent.
- Patient is diabetic — diabetics often present with atypical or muted symptoms.
- Pain is sudden and tearing (suggests aortic dissection).
- Pain is worse on inspiration with leg swelling (suggests pulmonary embolism).

## Differential diagnosis to consider

- Acute coronary syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI / unstable angina)
- Aortic dissection
- Pulmonary embolism
- Pericarditis or myocarditis
- Pneumothorax
- Esophageal spasm or GERD (lower priority but common confounder)
- Costochondritis (musculoskeletal — usually reproducible on palpation)

## Pre-visit signals to surface to the doctor

- Exact pain duration in minutes / hours.
- Whether the pain is constant, intermittent, exertional, or at rest.
- Any nitroglycerin use and response.
- Risk factors: diabetes, hypertension, smoking history, family history of early CAD.
- Current medications, especially anticoagulants and antihypertensives.

## Interpreter notes

When chest pain is suspected ACS, the interpreter should be ready for rapid medication terminology — nitroglycerin, aspirin, beta-blockers — and to convey a calm but urgent tone without alarming the patient. Pre-load signs for: heart attack, blood clot, blood pressure, stent, ECG.
