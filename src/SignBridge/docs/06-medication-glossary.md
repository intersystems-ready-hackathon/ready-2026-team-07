# Common Medications — Glossary for Interpreters

**Category:** glossary
**Condition:** medications
**Red flag:** false

This glossary lists common medications a deaf patient may mention or hear during a clinical visit. Each entry includes plain-English meaning and an interpreter cue.

## Cardiovascular

- **Aspirin** — blood thinner; used to prevent heart attack and stroke. Cue: "anti-clotting pill, not pain reliever in this context."
- **Nitroglycerin** — opens blood vessels; placed under the tongue for chest pain. Cue: "tongue spray for heart pain."
- **Metoprolol / Carvedilol** — beta-blockers; slow the heart and lower blood pressure.
- **Lisinopril** — ACE inhibitor; lowers blood pressure, may cause dry cough.
- **Atorvastatin / Rosuvastatin** — statins; lower cholesterol.
- **Apixaban / Rivaroxaban / Warfarin** — anticoagulants; blood thinners that prevent clots.

## Diabetes

- **Metformin** — first-line oral; lowers blood sugar.
- **Insulin** — injected hormone; lowers blood sugar quickly.
- **Ozempic (semaglutide)** — weekly injection; for diabetes and weight loss.
- **Jardiance (empagliflozin)** — daily pill; helps kidneys remove glucose.

## Respiratory

- **Albuterol** — rescue inhaler; opens airways quickly.
- **Symbicort / Advair** — daily controller inhaler.
- **Prednisone** — oral steroid; reduces inflammation.

## Pain and inflammation

- **Acetaminophen / Tylenol** — pain reliever, NOT anti-inflammatory.
- **Ibuprofen / Naproxen** — NSAIDs; pain and inflammation.
- **Gabapentin / Pregabalin** — nerve pain.

## Mental health

- **Sertraline / Escitalopram** — SSRIs; for depression and anxiety.
- **Bupropion** — atypical antidepressant; also used for smoking cessation.
- **Quetiapine** — antipsychotic; sometimes used off-label for sleep.

## Antibiotics

- **Amoxicillin / Augmentin** — common antibiotics for ear, sinus, throat infections.
- **Azithromycin** — short-course antibiotic for respiratory infections.
- **Ciprofloxacin** — for urinary or GI infections.
