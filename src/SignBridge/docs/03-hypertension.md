# Hypertension — Pre-Visit Notes

**Category:** cardiology
**Condition:** hypertension
**Red flag:** false

## Severity tiers (adult)

- **Normal:** systolic < 120 and diastolic < 80
- **Elevated:** systolic 120–129 and diastolic < 80
- **Stage 1:** systolic 130–139 OR diastolic 80–89
- **Stage 2:** systolic ≥ 140 OR diastolic ≥ 90
- **Hypertensive urgency:** systolic ≥ 180 or diastolic ≥ 120 with no end-organ damage
- **Hypertensive emergency:** systolic ≥ 180 or diastolic ≥ 120 WITH end-organ damage (chest pain, vision change, neuro deficit, severe headache, dyspnea) — this is ESI 1.

## When elevated BP becomes a red flag

A systolic reading at or above 180, or a diastolic reading at or above 120, accompanied by any of the following, is a hypertensive emergency:

- Severe headache or vision change.
- New chest pain or shortness of breath.
- Confusion, weakness, or numbness on one side.
- Pregnancy with proteinuria (preeclampsia).

## Common medications

- **ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril)** — watch for cough, hyperkalemia.
- **ARBs (losartan, valsartan)** — alternative when ACEi not tolerated.
- **Calcium channel blockers (amlodipine, diltiazem)** — peripheral edema common.
- **Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide)** — watch for hypokalemia.
- **Beta-blockers (metoprolol, carvedilol)** — bradycardia, fatigue.

## Pre-visit signals to surface

- Most recent home BP readings (last 7 days).
- Current antihypertensive regimen and adherence.
- Prior episodes of hypertensive urgency or emergency.
- Comorbid diabetes, CKD, or prior stroke.

## Interpreter notes

Pre-load signs for: blood pressure, hypertension, ACE inhibitor, diuretic, swelling, dizziness, kidney, headache, vision. Patients often confuse "high blood pressure" with "high cholesterol" — confirm which condition the doctor is addressing.
